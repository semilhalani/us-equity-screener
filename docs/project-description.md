# US Equity Screener and Scoring Engine: Project Description

## What this is

I built this to screen and rank pretty much the entire investable US stock market on my own terms, instead of relying on gut calls made one stock at a time. It is a personal project, built entirely in Google Sheets and Google Apps Script, that pulls fundamentals and market data from Finnhub, scrapes supplementary data from Finviz, benchmarks every stock against its own tier and peer group, and runs everything through a rule based scoring engine that outputs a 0 to 100 score and a plain verdict for each ticker. Four output sheets sit on top of that: a Buy List for long term picks, a Moonshot Watchlist for small speculative bets, an Earnings Alerts calendar, and an Insider Trading Feed.

It replaced an older, manually maintained tracker spreadsheet that had grown fragile and error prone. The goal was a system that is resumable, chunked to survive Apps Script's execution limits, and self healing when something goes wrong mid run, so it can reliably process thousands of tickers without me babysitting it.

## The decision problem behind the design

The system is not built around a single "which stock is best" number, because that hides too much. The real question changes shape depending on time horizon and what is actually true about a stock right now, so the system is structured around a handful of different lenses on the same underlying data instead of one score that tries to answer everything at once.

- A good business, fairly priced today: buy now.
- A good business that is expensive right now: watch it and wait for a pullback rather than chase it.
- A good business that is cheap right now: the best case entry, worth sizing up conviction on.
- A choppy, earnings driven stock: a separate, shorter horizon swing trade idea with its own risk rules.
- A small, speculative company with real asymmetric upside: a small, equal sized position, never sized up just because the story is exciting.

The first three of those live together on the Buy List, distinguished by an Entry_Timing field rather than three separate sheets, since they are really the same evaluation with price sitting in different places. The earnings driven idea gets its own Earnings Alerts sheet, since it is a genuinely different, event driven strategy. The speculative bets get their own Moonshot Watchlist, since they are scored on an entirely different formula from everything else.

## How the pipeline works

**Universe construction and tiering.** The system builds its own master ticker list rather than depending on a static file. It scrapes Finviz's stock screener across nine filters, three index memberships covering the S&P 500, NASDAQ 100, and Dow 30, and six market cap buckets covering Mega, Large, Mid, Small, Micro, and Nano, paginating through result pages until each filter is exhausted. Alongside that, it pulls Finnhub's full US symbol list for canonical ticker and company metadata, keeps only Common Stock, ADR, and EQS instrument types, and applies a whitelist based exchange filter that keeps NYSE, Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, NYSE American, Cboe BZX and BYX, and IEX, and drops OTC and pink sheet names that are not tradable through my own brokerage anyway. Every surviving ticker lands in one of four tiers: Tier1_Core for major index members, Tier2_LargeMid, Tier3_Small, and Tier4_Moonshot for micro and nano cap stocks. A second pass calls Finnhub's company profile endpoint per ticker to fill in precise market cap, IPO date, listing age in days, a new listing flag, and current shares outstanding.

**Fundamentals ingestion.** For every ticker, the system scrapes Finviz's individual stock page for sector, industry, and forward looking earnings date and session, and separately calls Finnhub's metrics endpoint for roughly two dozen financial ratios: 52 week high and low, trailing and industry or sector PE, EPS, book value, ROE now and three years ago, net profit margin, an ROIC figure labeled honestly as a proxy for true ROCE since Finnhub's free tier does not expose real ROCE, three and five year revenue and EPS growth alongside matching industry and sector benchmarks, debt to equity and debt to assets now and three years ago, and dividend per share and yield. A row only gets marked as successfully fetched, with a fresh timestamp, if both the Finviz scrape and the Finnhub call actually returned data. A partial failure leaves the ticker marked stale so it gets retried automatically on the next run instead of quietly sitting there with blank fields forever.

**Two layers of peer benchmarking.** Finviz's own industry and sector summary tables get scraped too, but only as a blended, cap agnostic reference and cross check, since they mix every market cap together and a mega cap trading at the same PE as a nano cap does not mean the same thing for both companies. The benchmarks actually used for scoring come from a separate step that groups every already fetched ticker by industry or sector and tier, and takes the median, not the mean, of PE, ROE, net margin, three year revenue growth, three year EPS growth, and debt to equity. PE is filtered to positive values only before taking the median, since a negative PE is not a meaningful reference point for what a fairly priced peer trades at. Any group with fewer than five tickers gets flagged as a low sample size, and both an industry level and a sector level benchmark are computed side by side, so scoring can fall back to the broader sector benchmark when an industry's sample is too thin.

**Disqualifier gates.** Before a stock is ever scored, it passes through five hard pass or fail checks that ask a different kind of question from the scoring engine. Scoring asks how good a stock is, on a scale. The gates ask whether there is something specifically disqualifying about it, a plain yes or no, and a failed gate overrides everything else no matter how good the rest of the picture looks. The five gates are liquidity, listing age, dilution, zero revenue quarters for Moonshot tier stocks only, and cash runway for Moonshot tier stocks only. The full detail on how each one works lives in `tiers-gates-and-scoring.md`, alongside the scoring formulas.

**Earnings history and the earnings behavior engine.** For every ticker, the system fetches up to four recent quarters of EPS estimate, actual, and surprise data from Finnhub, and computes two scores from it. Volatility_Score is a recency weighted average of the absolute size of the surprise, regardless of direction, so a high score just means big swings either way. Reliability_Score tracks direction instead, so a high score means consistent beats and a low score means consistent misses. A separate categorization function buckets each ticker into one of fifteen labeled categories based on the pattern of surprises across however many quarters of real history exist, rather than hardcoding an assumption of exactly four quarters, so a recent IPO with partial history still gets categorized correctly.

**Insider trading tracking.** The system pulls Finnhub's Form 4 insider transaction feed per ticker, keeps a 370 day trailing window, and de duplicates against previously recorded rows using ticker, insider name, transaction date, and transaction code together, since this data is stored as an append only log rather than updated in place. From that raw log it derives a Buy_Signal_Score, the trailing twelve month net dollar value of open market purchases normalized against the ticker's own market cap, and a Sell_Urgency_Score, the trailing ninety day net value sold, normalized the same way, with a bonus when two or more distinct insiders sold in the same window. That clustering is an imperfect but real proxy for coordinated selling versus a routine, pre scheduled sale, since Finnhub's free tier does not expose insider seniority or scheduling information.

**Scoring.** The full mechanics live in `tiers-gates-and-scoring.md`, but the short version: everything except Moonshot tier gets a Quality_Score, Value_Score, and Growth_Score against tier aware peer benchmarks, rolled up into a Fundamentals_Score with weights that shift by tier. Moonshot tier stocks get a completely separate Moonshot_Score built from growth, margin, and debt trend instead. A benchmark comparison step explicitly returns a neutral 50, not a blank and not a crash, when no reliable benchmark exists for a stock's industry, sector, and tier combination, which is exactly why the Buy List requires Quality_Score, Value_Score, and Growth_Score to all be genuinely real numbers before including a ticker, not just a passing Fundamentals_Score.

**The Buy List and the other output sheets.** The Buy List filters down to qualified, non Moonshot tickers with a strong or watch verdict, sorts by Fundamentals_Score, and writes three GOOGLEFINANCE driven columns, current price, percent of 52 week range, and an entry timing label, so those three numbers stay live between script runs without costing any API quota. The Moonshot Watchlist filters to qualified Moonshot tier tickers with a real Moonshot_Score, sorted descending. Earnings Alerts cross references the scraped forward looking earnings date against the Scores sheet and lists anything reporting within the next fourteen days, sorted soonest first. The Insider Feed lists individual open market purchase and sale transactions from the last thirty days above a materiality floor, newest first, with a cluster flag for coordinated activity. All four are covered in more depth in `presentation-sheets.md`.

## Engineering principles I followed throughout

A few conventions repeat across every module in this project, since they came out of real bugs I hit early on and did not want to repeat.

Every refresh script reprocesses only tickers that are missing or past a staleness threshold, tracked through a hidden scratch sheet rather than Script Properties, since Script Properties has a roughly nine kilobyte per value limit that a list of thousands of tickers would blow straight past. Every refresh script also has a small test variant that processes a handful of tickers and, where it makes sense, writes to an isolated test sheet rather than the real one.

Incomplete or unavailable data always gets an explicit status flag rather than a guess or a silent default. A missing dilution history is labeled insufficient history, not left blank with an ambiguous meaning, and a gate that never ran is not the same as a gate that ran and passed. Any new external data source gets checked against two or three real tickers by hand before being trusted at full scale, which is exactly how the dilution gate's threshold got validated against real tickers before it went live.

Raw sheets and the Scores sheet hold clean numbers, booleans, and short codes only. Plain English sentences and formatting live in the presentation sheets, generated from that clean data, never duplicated as stored text further upstream. This split came out of an earlier version where a single Action column tried to pack a verdict, its reasoning, and caution sentences into one string, which was slow to read and nearly impossible to filter or sort on downstream.

## Automation and reliability

Apps Script kills any execution after six minutes with no way to extend it, and a full run across the roughly 5,300 tickers that currently pass the universe filters takes far longer than that. Every data fetching stage is built on a shared chunked execution framework instead: a time driven trigger fires a worker once a minute, each invocation processes a bounded slice of pending tickers by reading its own slice directly from a sheet rather than storing a large payload anywhere, and progress is tracked as a simple index and total in Script Properties. An hourly safety net trigger recreates the per minute worker if it ever gets deleted by an Apps Script quota hiccup, and a LockService based lock stops overlapping trigger firings from racing on the same shared progress state.

A shared fetch utility wraps every HTTP call with realistic browser headers, exponential backoff retries up to four attempts, and fails fast on a 404 or 410 instead of retrying, since those are permanent errors and retrying them just wastes time across an entire universe of obscure or OTC tickers Finviz simply has no page for. Finnhub calls rotate across eight API keys in round robin fashion to spread call volume across multiple free tier quotas.

The project has also run into and worked around Google Sheets' hard ten million cell ceiling once already. The root cause turned out to be compound: one sheet was allocated far more columns than it actually used, several sheets still carried row allocation left over from an earlier, much larger universe before the OTC filter was added, since clearing a sheet's contents does not shrink its allocated grid, and an older method for tracking share count history was quietly adding thousands of rows on every single run for no real benefit. The fix was trimming the unused allocation and retiring that history method in favor of computing dilution directly from SEC filings instead, which gives the same answer immediately rather than after months of accumulated snapshots.

## Known limitations

This is a rule based, deterministic system today. There is no machine learning, no statistical model fitting, and no neural network anywhere in the current code. Moving toward that is a stated future direction, not something built yet, and the code does not explain why it started rule based rather than framing it as a deliberate philosophy, so that is genuinely an open question rather than something I am glossing over.

A few other honest gaps worth naming. There is no backtesting yet: scoring weights and verdict thresholds are marked in the code itself as starting points that have not been checked against real outcomes, and a Track Record sheet name is reserved in the configuration but nothing populates it. A few metrics are honest stand ins for something better, ROIC in place of true ROCE, and EPS surprise magnitude in place of actual price swings around earnings, since Finnhub's price candle endpoint is not available on the free tier for US stocks. The Finviz scraping works off regex extraction against Finviz's HTML rather than a stable API, so an upstream markup change can break it, caught only by defensive parsing and logging rather than prevented outright. Running the full pipeline is still a manual, ordered process rather than one orchestrated command. And a Post Earnings Moves sheet, meant to flag overreactions after a report, is still not built, since it depends on a forward only earnings date log that only becomes useful once enough real history accumulates going forward, and it cannot be backfilled for quarters already reported.

## Where this could go next

A backtesting engine is the most natural next step, since the scoring thresholds are already marked as unvalidated and a Track Record sheet name is already reserved. It would snapshot scores and verdicts over time and correlate them against subsequent price performance using the same GOOGLEFINANCE mechanism the Buy List already relies on.

Since fundamentals, earnings surprise history, and insider signals are already computed as structured per ticker features on a recurring schedule, that data is already shaped like a feature store that could feed a machine learning model for price movement or earnings surprise prediction, which lines up with where I eventually want to take this.

Adding sentiment analysis on earnings calls or news would slot in naturally alongside the existing score components, using the same benchmark relative scoring pattern already established. A portfolio level view that considers sector concentration or correlation across the Buy List, rather than ranking every ticker independently, is a direct extension given that sector and industry are already first class fields on every ticker. Moving core historical data to a real database is also worth doing eventually, since the project has already had to actively engineer around the Sheets cell ceiling once. And a lightweight alerting layer on top of the existing trigger infrastructure, an email or a webhook firing when a ticker newly enters the Buy List or a cluster insider selling event shows up, would be a small, natural extension of automation that is already running on a schedule.

## Tools, technologies, and skills involved

Google Apps Script and the V8 runtime, Google Sheets used as a structured multi sheet application data layer with live formula driven data through GOOGLEFINANCE, REST API integration against Finnhub for symbol lookup, company profiles, financial metrics, SEC filings data, earnings history, and insider transactions, HTML scraping through regex extraction against Finviz, API key rotation and rate limit management across multiple credentials, time driven trigger orchestration and chunking to work within a serverless execution time ceiling, concurrency control through LockService, resumable and idempotent pipeline design, rule based multi factor scoring and financial ranking logic, quantitative financial analysis covering valuation, growth, profitability, leverage, dilution, and cash runway, peer group median benchmarking with sample size honesty and outlier resistance, defensive data quality engineering with explicit sentinel values instead of ambiguous blanks, retry and backoff logic throughout, workbook scale resource management against a hard platform limit, and a presentation layer cleanly separated from raw data storage.
